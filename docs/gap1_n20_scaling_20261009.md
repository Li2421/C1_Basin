# Gap1 scaling replacement: N=2, 10, 20

On 2026-10-09 the requested scaling endpoint changed from N=50 to N=20.
N=50 eta work was cancelled before its first seed finished. The accepted
N=50 frozen Flow and its earlier opposing evidence remain archived, but no
further N=50 eta rollout is planned. N=2 and N=10 frozen controllers are
unchanged.

N=20 has no pre-existing checkpoint or demonstration dataset. Its Flow will be
trained from scratch; no N=50 or N=10 weights will be resized or transferred.
The physical plant, robot radius, dynamics, 0.62 m Gap1 opening, accepted
hard safety projection, and later OrthoFlow3 eta semantics remain unchanged.
The N=20 horizon is 16,000 steps, matching the established `800*N` scaling.

The first N=20 data collection (`n20_competence_data_v1`) was stopped before
dataset finalization when an old `solo` selector was found to select a suffix
of the left-agent array rather than one agent. Its preflight and partial log
are retained as an aborted audit. The selector is fixed to a length-one
indexed array and verified for all 20 case indices. A new, outcome-blind
`n20_competence_data_v2` collection has been submitted as Slurm job 9620.
It requests 48 independent TRAIN and 12 DEV physical seeds, five non-opposing
modes (`solo`, `five`, `half`, `parked_half`, `full`), two row permutations, and
paired x-reflected trajectories. Each accepted pair contributes equal L→R
and R→L trajectories and transitions. Every demonstration must terminate
with all 20 agents at their goals; gate crossing alone is insufficient.
The generator performs the shared rollout-cache preflight first.

The from-scratch N=20 set-attention Flow training job 9647 depends on job
9620 succeeding. Its 20,000-step schedule uses active-agent normalization,
early-state and near-goal sampling, and balanced direction data. It does not
contain opposing traffic or priority demonstrations. Checkpoint choice will
use only DEV non-opposing controls. Fresh non-conflicting acceptance and the
original opposing test remain closed until a candidate is selected, then the
checkpoint is frozen before opposing evaluation.

CPU projection timing on the same initial-state snapshot, with the unchanged
hard projection applied twice per step, was 5.8 ms at N=20 versus 26.6 ms
at N=50 (50 repeated calls each). This is a timing probe, not an end-to-end
rollout benchmark. Together with the 16,000 versus 40,000 step horizons, it
suggests N=20 labeling can be roughly an order of magnitude faster than N=50;
the conservative end-to-end estimate is 2–4 days including new Flow data,
training, validation, opposing audit, and exact Q16 labels. The latest
resource instruction reserves twelve of the 24 CPU slots for another task.
Our simultaneous Slurm CPU allocation must therefore stay at or below twelve,
including N=20 collection/training and the ongoing N=2/N=10 eta arrays. This
supersedes the earlier 24-slot allowance and the 2026-10-10 09:00 reduction to
nineteen. On 2026-10-09 the active allocation was reduced to twelve: N=2 at
most six, N=10 at most five, and N=20 collection one. N=10 array task 185 was
canceled while reducing the running allocation and queued as dependent
recovery job 10024; its partial atomic per-seed results remain reusable.
Before releasing N=20 training (four CPU slots), the eta-array throttles
must be reduced so their combined maximum stays at most eight.

The outcome-blind N=20 evaluation schedule is fixed before training finishes:
DEV checkpoint selection uses four fresh physical states per non-opposing mode
with master seed 162020; pre-freeze acceptance uses twelve different DEV
states per mode with master seed 398201. The eight modes are single-even,
single-odd, few-even, few-odd, full one-way in both directions, and temporal
release with either side first. The original opposing task is reserved for
TEST split, master seed 2026102012, twelve independent states, and opens
only after the selected checkpoint is frozen. Seed/physical-state overlap
with training demonstrations and the later eta split was checked from the
predeclared RNG policies: all pairwise seed intersections are empty. Physical
state hashes will be checked again once both datasets are materialized.

Candidate snapshots are ranked on the selection set by (1) zero collision
and numerical failures, (2) the worst per-mode complete-success count,
(3) total complete successes, (4) mean final goal occupancy, then (5)
smaller mean safety-projection correction. Offline validation loss is a
tie-breaker only. The separate twelve-case-per-mode acceptance set is opened
once for the selected candidate, not used to rank snapshots.
Each N=20 training snapshot may be screened in its raw sampling form and,
only if the paired DEV observation/action reflection audit is exact, with
the same N=20 weights under x-reflection-averaged sampling. This is a
predeclared Flow-only symmetry adapter, not a transferred N=50 model,
safety change, or opposing-traffic rule.

Pre-freeze acceptance requires at least 11/12 complete, collision-free
successes in each of the eight fresh categories, zero collisions and
zero numerical failures,
no severe directional asymmetry, and full-goal occupancy on successful
dense episodes. Gate crossing alone cannot pass. N=20 becomes READY_FOR_ETA
only if the frozen opposing evaluation also yields a meaningful,
collision-free coordination gridlock regime under the unchanged safety
projection.

The N=2/N=10 eta work already in progress remains useful. Its exact physical
state and eta identities will be reused in a new N=2/10/20 design via the
shared rollout cache after the N=20 prerequisite passes. No N=20 eta data
will be generated before frozen Flow competence and opposing gridlock are
established.
