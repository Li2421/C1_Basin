FINAL VERDICT: STATE_CONDITIONED_ADAPTATION_CONFIRMED
On 256 independently drawn fresh physical states, frozen Q(s,eta) improves held-out success over Q(eta), the discovery-selected best fixed eta, and every one of the 16 fixed-eta policies. 9. INTERPRETATION AND EVIDENCE BOUNDARY
Primary natural distribution: Q512 93.54% versus Qeta/discovery-best-fixed 88.33%; paired physical-state difference +5.21 percentage points, 95% bootstrap CI [+2.65,+7.88]. Three frozen optimizer fits individually gain +5.47,+5.66,+4.49 points.
All-16 fixed falsification: evaluation-best fixed eta is index 13 at 88.33%; adaptive minus the maximum fixed policy on each bootstrap resample is +5.21 points, simultaneous 95% CI [+2.64,+7.94]. Thus even post-hoc selection of a single fixed eta cannot reproduce the observed natural-distribution result.
Discovery-defined 3/8 adaptive-demand group has 38 states with preferred eta indices 1 (30), 4 (5), and 10 (3). Held-out success is 79.06% versus 33.22% for the predeclared fixed eta13, difference +45.83 points, paired state 95% CI [+36.62,+54.82]. The same Q512 policy was applied without subgroup tuning.
All 39 discovery-defined strong reversal pairs were included, covering 254 states and 4,547 pair-state cases; evaluation confirmed 4,423, contradicted 24, and left 100 ambiguous. Across 4,447 decisive evaluation cases, Q512 pairwise preference accuracy is 92.31% (state bootstrap CI [91.21,93.38]), exceeding Qeta by +3.76 points (paired state CI [+2.57,+4.92]) and 32-state Q by +5.22 points ([+4.17,+6.32]).
Actual top-1 eta selection uses indices 1,4,8,10,13 across the three fits (entropy 1.72-1.87 bits; different choices for two random states 66.9-70.4%). In discovery-preference group 1, eta1 is chosen 47.3% of fit-state decisions; in group 4, eta4 is chosen 49.8%; in group 13, eta13 is chosen 50.0%. The group-linked change plus the all-fixed contrast support a state-dependent selection mechanism.
Across all reversal side-cases, adaptive minus fixed eta13 is positive on both directions: +5.34 points (state CI [+2.59,+8.21]) and +2.67 points ([+0.43,+5.17]). This is aggregate evidence, not a claim that every eta pair benefits on both sides.
32-state diagnostic: held-out all-state success 85.07%, below both 512-state Q (93.54%) and fixed eta13 (88.33%); Q512 minus Q32 is +8.46 points, state CI [+6.14,+11.08]. Its independent-evaluation reversal accuracy is 87.09% versus Q512 92.31%.
Boundary: the strong-reversal state cohort includes 254/256 states and is not an independently concentrated hard subset; pairwise ranking accuracy is high even for a fixed selector because many comparisons involve clearly poor eta candidates. Only 1/39 reversal pairs shows positive Q512-minus-fixed gain on both sides separately, and the discovery-support representative pair does not. On the 38-state adaptive-demand subset, hindsight fixed eta1 reaches 83.55%, above Q512 79.06% (adaptive minus hindsight fixed -4.50 points, max-over-16 CI [-9.10,+0.11]); this does not overturn the positive all-state simultaneous all-fixed CI but limits any subgroup-universal claim.
Boundary: 8 evaluation seeds per state-eta give finite-seed Monte Carlo estimates; 296/65,536 outcomes are numerical unknowns and remain bounded, never imputed as failures or successes. The finding applies to this Ring global-development distribution, fixed FF pipeline and 16-candidate eta pool.
TEST discovery defines groups and fixed policy before opening its independent evaluation half; subgroup analysis is not a prospectively trained policy.
Eight evaluation seeds estimate success probabilities with Monte Carlo noise; finite-seed oracle is optimistic.
Physical state, not state-eta pair or rollout, is the bootstrap unit.
No new critic training, controller change, Flow change, safety change, eta-pool change, or targeted state sampling.
