# Toy K=16 critic: root-cause adjudication

**Verdict: `UNDERRESOLVED_BETWEEN_JOINT_DATA_SUPPORT_AND_MODEL_EXTRAPOLATION`.** The 13 lost states are real critic-selection failures (194/200 oracle versus 181/200 selected), not generator-coverage failures. Evidence favors sparse joint \((h,\eta)\) support plus overconfident extrapolation, amplified by max-over-16. Existing data do **not** cleanly prove that the 214-D input omits decisive physical information, nor that the network ignored sufficiently close low-Q training labels.

| Mechanism | Supporting evidence | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Joint data/support gap | Only 48 TRAIN states. Of 549 exact TRAIN eta values, 386 occur on just one state; only 40 occur on all 48. 76.5% of generated proposals are >0.10 normalized eta distance from any TRAIN eta. The 13 false top-1 have median nearest joint distance 0.553. None has a TRAIN pair at both state RMS distance ≤0.25 and eta distance ≤0.05. | This strict local support is also absent for 180/181 correct selections. False-vs-correct nearest joint distance 0.553 vs 0.470, exploratory Mann–Whitney p=0.085. Marginal eta distance is *not* higher for errors (0.132 vs 0.142). Thus support gap is a broad precondition, not by itself a specific classifier of error states. |
| 2. Systematic overconfident extrapolation | Five severe false top-1 have Q16 ≤5/16. In particular, episode 102 has 0/16 at predicted 0.973; episode 115 has 3/16 at predicted ~1. Five-critic agreement above 0.9 occurs on eight of the 13 errors. TRAIN MAE is 0.026, ordinary unseen-state+eta TEST MAE 0.123. In the \(p>0.95\) bin, B15 precision drops from 99.2% TRAIN to 96.1% ordinary TEST, 94.4% all generator proposals, and 93.4% selected top-1. A 5-NN distance-weighted local estimate gives median 0.618 on the 13 bad proposals versus critic median ~0.973. | kNN itself selects only 157/200 B15, so it is not a better policy. No severe false positive has a genuinely near, precise low-Q TRAIN label; episode 115's nearest 20 TRAIN rows are all Q=1 (though not very near). This does not prove that existing data were sufficient and only fitting failed. |
| 3. Max-selection amplification | K=8→16 adds 10 oracle-covered states (184→194) but only four critic successes (177→181); failures *among coverable states* rise 7→13. Twelve K16 selected states have \(p>0.95\) yet are non-B15, versus seven at K8. Eleven of the 13 error winners come from proposal indices 8–15. | Max-selection explains why errors matter more at larger K, not why Q was overestimated in the first place. Aggregate upper-tail calibration deterioration is modest, not a catastrophic global failure. |

## What the direct checks rule out—and do not rule out

- **No identified code-path bug.** All 76 structured-panel true-t0 features replay from the frozen state files and authoritative batched Flow call to ≤\(10^{-15}\) absolute difference. All 3,200 frozen critic logits replay from the three checkpoint files to ≤\(9.5\times10^{-6}\). The same 214-D feature ordering, stored h normalization, eta affine normalization, and sigmoid-of-mean-logit decoding are used. Every one of the 2,876 canonical TRAIN pair counts matches valid exact seed-level database rows; no stale/conflicting aggregate was found in this set. The Toy environment, Flow hash, OrthoFlow3 hash, and safety config agree. TRAIN and hard cohort have different RNG namespaces and therefore different strict cache controller UIDs; this is an evaluation distribution difference, not evidence of a control-code mismatch.
- **Representation aliasing is not established.** The 214-D h contains positions, velocities, goals, first Flow/safety actions, barrier/projection state, progress history and monitor state. In TRAIN same-exact-eta pairs with at least eight trials, no \(\lvert\Delta Q\rvert\ge0.75\) conflict occurs below normalized h RMS 0.20; two occur below 0.30. The hard errors lack truly near labelled TRAIN comparators, so a missing hidden variable or sharp unobserved interaction cannot be excluded. Nearest-TRAIN-state h distance is only mildly larger for errors than correct selections (median 0.460 vs 0.399); it is below the ordinary VAL median of 0.474.
- **Finite Q16 noise does not explain the severe cases.** Four errors are borderline 14/16 and deserve caution, but the five Q≤5/16 selected points have exact 16-seed records. Under a true Q=0.9 model, seeing at most 0/16, 3/16 or 5/16 has binomial probability \(10^{-16}\), \(4.2\times10^{-11}\), or \(2.7\times10^{-8}\), respectively. The user's illustrative 1/16 case is episode 177 and its current critic prediction is 0.764, not near one; the 0/16 and 3/16 cases are the actual near-one examples. No compatible extra Q32/Q64 record was found for these exact state–eta–controller keys.
- **Capacity alone is not a settled explanation.** A small pure-NLL controlled check (same VAL-only checkpoint rule) gives median hard B15 167/200 for a smaller network, 180/200 for the current size, 184/200 for a moderately larger one, across seeds 17/23/41. The model can benefit somewhat from capacity, but this does not identify the local false-positive mechanism; no architecture was selected from hard outcomes. A 25%→50%→100% data-edge check gives median 167→180→180, with substantial seed variance. These are diagnostic only.

## Missing decisive evidence and minimal next test

The missing object is a **small, matched local state×eta Q matrix around the five severe false positives**, not another model score. Freeze two tiny outcome-blind initial-state perturbations for each of episodes 102, 115, 162, 176 and 177; at each perturbation evaluate the already-frozen critic-bad eta and one known B15-good eta with the standard 16 matched seeds. This is at most 320 requested continuations before cache reuse. Preflight the global database first; do not execute as part of this audit. If physically/feature-near states show sharply conflicting Q, inspect whether h resolves that change (representation/basin-boundary question). If Q is locally stable and low around the bad eta while the frozen critic stays near one, the case for model extrapolation bias strengthens. A subsequent train-only local-supervision test, with separate held-out perturbations, would distinguish learnable structure from irreducible aliasing.
