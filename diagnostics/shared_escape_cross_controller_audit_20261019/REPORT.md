# C1 / OrthoFlow3 shared escape: cross-controller exploitability audit

**Verdict:** A controller-independent safe reference structure is **PROVEN** on the archived Toy local yield/pass domains, and a separate coordinated-rotation reference class is **PROVEN** for a small open set of Ring states. Whether original Goal/Perp/Rel under **TT** exploits either structure on real unseen controller trajectories is **UNKNOWN**. The proposed `η†` has a valid **one-step sufficient certificate** after correcting the regularized projection identity. It has no demonstrated coverage on eligible real unseen controllers, and the one-step implication cannot establish complete escape.

The missing named draft files were searched and absent. This audit preserves the original family, parameter box, controller code, safety projectors and historical outcomes. It reuses old numerical evidence, adds only a no-rollout CBF reference-row check for Ring, and writes all new artifacts here. `THEOREM_AUDIT.md` contains the formal statement and proof; `DATA_INVENTORY.md` identifies frozen source/field compatibility.

The Toy `EXPLOITABILITY_METRICS.csv` is an auditable extraction of 68 saved action rows: 34 TT, 34 FF; 17 selected state/key records. It computes `d_c` and the **raw** `η†` correction from saved anchors/B without new model inference. Among the 17 TT witness rows at `(0.5,0,.5)`, 11 have both executed progress coordinates above `10⁻⁸ m/s`, but **0/68 rows lie in the predeclared `K_entry` or `K_pass`**. The 11/17 figure is a selected, partly duplicated *action-panel observation*, not the coverage of `η†`, a success rate, an episode count, or evidence that its signs would persist. Every `r_m`, η† executed action and episode-outcome field is left blank where not computed; no value is imputed.

The main failure to make an RSS-level universal claim is **missing mechanism linkage**, with a concrete distribution gap: Toy's certified K domains and real action samples do not overlap; Ring has a nonempty common structure, but its present certified initial class covers **0/24** cross-type 1c new test states even before checking phase. This is zero coverage of this *narrow reference class*, not zero Ring escape feasibility. Its action set has not been matched to historical conflict states or to TT's reachable set. The pair-contact counterexample shows that safety projection can suppress passing at a geometric boundary. Existing data do **not** identify how much of remaining failure is expression of the three columns, projection suppression, temporal stage loss, or selector error. A selected menu failure would not identify continuous-box infeasibility. Unknown, numerical failures, timeouts and missing outcomes are never relabeled as deadlock.

For paper wording, the strongest currently defensible central *theory* contribution is a **conditional, statewise exploitability certificate** plus separate controller-independent local reference constructions, with explicit counterexamples to unconditional progress preservation. A claim that the same escape structure is actually exploitable across unseen controllers as the main demonstrated result is **UNKNOWN** until action-level coverage and trajectory continuation are measured on a matched Ring or Toy protocol. Historical Ring success improvements support motivation and endpoint effectiveness, but do not fill that proof-to-execution link.

`SUMMARY_BY_CONTROLLER.csv` was intentionally not created: there are zero stage-eligible Toy rows and no Ring action-level eligibility mapping. Any per-controller table of `r_m>d_c` coverage now would be fabricated. Historical endpoint tables remain in their original reports with their original clustered intervals and holdout semantics.
