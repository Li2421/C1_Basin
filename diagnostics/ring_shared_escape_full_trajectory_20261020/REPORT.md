# Ring shared escape: archived full-trajectory audit

**Answer to Q1: FAILED for the previously proved strict Ring class.** Across the saved round27, round29, cross-type 1c and cross-type 3 panels, **0/73,358 recorded episodes** can visit the frozen rotation domain at any saved step. All **32,962,736 saved state timepoints** are excluded. This is a mathematical exclusion from the fixed goals: that domain requires each goal within `.05 m` of some radius-`2.5 m` ideal square target, whereas every source state has at least one goal whose radius differs from `2.5 m` by more than `.05 m`. Goals remain fixed during each episode. Every trajectory file exists, and its position-array header, step count, and stored goals were checked against the frozen record. The multi-gigabyte position arrays did not need decompression to establish this all-step result. Numerical-failure traces are counted only through their last saved state. This is zero coverage of **this precise class**, not zero Ring coordination feasibility.

The round27 and round29 panels reuse the same 32 initial states; the four panels contain **104 distinct state UIDs**, not 136 independent initial states. Cross-type 1c Flow uses FF; its other eight nominal types use TT. Cross-type 3's saved new evaluation uses TT, including transformer BC, gradient MPC and conflict-based search as three whole-type critic holdouts; this is not a claim that the entire generation pipeline had never seen related controller behavior. Round27 uses native TT, including its Flow baseline; round29's `no_safety` and `hard_safety` chains are not correction-TT arms. `TRAJECTORY_COVERAGE.csv` gives one row per episode with the controller, arm, seed key, η schedule, outcome, trace verification, exclusion margin, and stage fields. Initial coverage, ever-visited episode fraction, and step-timepoint fraction are each **0** in every controller type and in both successful and unsuccessful recorded episodes. A step fraction is only an auxiliary archive census; steps are not independent observations. Trace errors/unknowns were retained separately from collision, timeout and success.

Round26 `diagnostics/method_benchmark_v1/` was also checked: its reconstructed Ring/other-scene arrays contain labels, predictions and method choices, but no compatible full position/action trace bundle. It is excluded from the trajectory denominator; its development comparisons are not relabeled as a new held-out geometry test.

Data recovery audit: round27/29 traces preserve positions, velocities, executed actions, goals and swept clearance; 1c/3 additionally preserve nominal actions. Each raw record retains state/controller/η/seed identities, the switch schedule, trace path and original outcome. The frozen runtimes derive per-step keys from the archived state namespace, seed and physical step. **Precorrection safety anchor and B columns are not stored**, and `safe_actions` in 1c/3 is the executed action array, not that anchor. Exact reconstruction would require a checkpoint/runtime and original-key replay gate; it was not performed here. No arbitrary replacement key was generated.

**Answer to Q2: UNKNOWN for actual exploitation of the old domain.** There are no eligible states, so `u_TT∈E_rot`, menu coverage, `r_m`, `d_c`, and stage continuation have a zero denominator. `TT_EXPLOITABILITY.csv` was therefore not created. The zero coverage does not mean TT failed at eligible states. It means this old certificate cannot explain the archived improvements across Controllers.

An independent **exploratory** Ring candidate was specified from the annular task, generator bounds and full safety geometry before scanning either new evaluation's actions; its exact frozen definition is in `CANDIDATE_SPEC.json`. It admits either common clockwise or common counterclockwise circulation with per-agent tangential progress and bounded radial motion. A common-rotation reference is **PROVEN** safe and reaches a geometric angular stage within 628 steps on its stated initial class, using all 48+48 polygon segments, six pair barriers and speed balls. On the 24 cross-type 1c new states, both signs of its initial reference have positive margins on all 390 CBF rows; the minimum numerical row slack is **0.347945**. The 48 cross-type 3 states have minimum slack **0.340246**. Wall rows have velocity units; pair rows have squared-distance-rate units. These finite checks are **NUMERICALLY CERTIFIED**. The candidate covers **24/24** and **48/48** initial states geometrically. This confirms nonempty reference feasibility on those states, not realized correction.

Actual selected cross-type 1c TT trajectories show a sharper limit. Among **2,304** paired `self_schedule` TT episodes (eight types; 24 states, two seeds per base instance), **374** contain at least one step in the candidate's simultaneous four-agent action cone, and **2,662/233,700** geometrically eligible steps satisfy it. All 2,662 such steps satisfy the recomputed original 390 CBF rows and speed balls to `1e−7` tolerance and have nonnegative saved swept clearance. Yet **2,233** of those episodes succeed, including **1,874 successes with no candidate-cone step at all**. A separate common-direction *angular exit* appears in **1,963/2,304** episodes; it is a geometric trajectory observation and does not show that the candidate action cone drove the exit. Under the paired safety-baseline TT arms, the corresponding counts are 204/2,304 episodes with a candidate action, 2,477/146,520 eligible action steps, 662 angular exits, and 357 successes.

The cross-type 3 panel has the same limitation: selected `self_schedule` TT has **70/3,744** episodes with any candidate-cone step, **263/139,912** eligible action steps in it (all pass the 390-row recomputation), **2,273** angular exits and **3,147** successes. The three whole-type critic holdouts are especially informative: transformer BC has **396/576** successes and 47 episodes with a candidate action; gradient MPC has **562/576** successes and **zero** candidate actions; conflict-based search has **506/576** successes and 6 candidate-action episodes. These counts do not show that correction caused each success; gradient MPC's safety-only baseline already succeeds often. The FF Flow subset from 1c is tabulated separately in `CANDIDATE_SUMMARY.json`. All counts are descriptive for archived panels, without a new causal contrast or population confidence interval.

**Answers to Q3–Q4:** The old domain fails because its goal-radius tolerance excludes the benchmark's actual goals, even when its polygon/pair safety proof remains sound. This is a certificate/distribution mismatch. The broader candidate's safe reference exists, while the actual successful TT paths rarely put all four robots into the chosen simultaneous tangential cone; staggered motion, asymmetric yielding, or a different coordination description may be needed. The saved 1c traces contain positions, nominal actions and final actions, but not the precorrection safety anchor or B. Neither the original three columns' counterfactual menu capacity nor anchor suppression, QP suppression, or selector error is identified by these counts. The fully recomputed CBF rows certify **the observed candidate-positive actions**, not untried menu actions. One-step membership is not a full escape guarantee. `FAILURE_ATTRIBUTION.md` keeps these distinctions explicit.

**Answer to Q5: UNKNOWN pending one matched action audit.** The most valuable small validation is to predeclare a stage that allows staggered Ring coordination, then reconstruct the original TT anchor/B and frozen menu at a bounded set of already saved state/key/controller tuples, first checking exact action replay. A controller-independent feasible action certificate, positive coverage on independent Controller instances, and sustained stage exit on the same episodes would bridge local existence to an actual cross-Controller mechanism. The present broad candidate is exploratory; its high geometric coverage alone does not establish that bridge. See `NEXT_DECISION.md`.
